1. Introduction to Macroeconomics II

Macroeconomics II studies advanced macroeconomic theories related to inflation, unemployment, output, labor markets, expectations, and economic policy.

  • Studies advanced macroeconomic models
  • Focuses on inflation and unemployment
  • Analyzes economic fluctuations and policies

2. Classical Economics

Classical economics believes that markets work efficiently and automatically move toward full employment equilibrium.

  • Economy is self-adjusting
  • Prices and wages are flexible
  • Government intervention is unnecessary

3. Assumptions of Classical Theory

Classical economists made several assumptions about markets and employment.

  • Perfect competition exists
  • Wages and prices are flexible
  • Full employment is normal
  • Savings automatically become investment

4. Sayâ€™s Law of Markets

Sayâ€™s Law states that supply creates its own demand.

  • Production generates income
  • Income creates demand
  • General overproduction is impossible

5. Classical View on Employment

Classical economists believed unemployment is temporary and voluntary.

  • Flexible wages restore employment
  • Labor market reaches equilibrium automatically
  • Government intervention not required

6. Keynesian Economics

Keynesian economics was developed by John Maynard Keynes during the Great Depression.

  • Aggregate demand determines output
  • Government intervention is important
  • Unemployment can persist

7. Keynesian Aggregate Demand

Keynes emphasized aggregate demand as the key determinant of output and employment.

  • Consumption affects demand
  • Investment drives growth
  • Low demand causes recession

8. Keynesian Consumption Function

The consumption function explains relationship between income and consumption.

  • Consumption rises with income
  • Savings also increase with income
  • MPC is important in analysis

9. Multiplier Effect

The multiplier effect explains how initial spending creates larger increase in national income.

  • Higher MPC creates larger multiplier
  • Investment increases income
  • Used in fiscal policy analysis

10. Keynesian Unemployment

Keynes believed unemployment occurs due to insufficient aggregate demand.

  • Demand deficiency reduces output
  • Firms reduce employment
  • Government spending can increase jobs

11. Monetarism

Monetarism was developed mainly by Milton Friedman. It emphasizes the role of money supply in economy.

  • Money supply affects inflation
  • Stable money growth is important
  • Government intervention should be limited

12. Quantity Theory of Money

Monetarists support the quantity theory of money.

  • Increase in money supply increases prices
  • Inflation is mainly a monetary phenomenon
  • Money affects nominal variables

13. Monetarist View on Inflation

Monetarists believed inflation results from excessive money supply growth.

  • Central bank controls inflation
  • Stable monetary policy is important
  • Money growth should be controlled

14. New Classical Economics

New classical economics emphasizes rational expectations and market clearing.

  • People use all available information
  • Markets adjust quickly
  • Government policy has limited effects

15. Rational Expectations Theory

People form expectations using all available information and economic understanding.

  • Economic agents are forward-looking
  • Policy effects are anticipated
  • Systematic policy becomes ineffective

16. Policy Ineffectiveness Proposition

New classical economists argued predictable policies cannot influence output permanently.

  • People anticipate policies
  • Output returns to natural level
  • Only unexpected policies affect output

17. New Keynesian Economics

New Keynesian economics combines Keynesian ideas with microeconomic foundations.

  • Markets may not adjust quickly
  • Prices and wages can be sticky
  • Government policy can stabilize economy

18. Sticky Prices and Sticky Wages

New Keynesians argue prices and wages adjust slowly.

  • Menu costs prevent price changes
  • Contracts make wages rigid
  • Markets may remain in disequilibrium

19. Efficiency Wage Theory

Firms may pay wages above equilibrium to improve productivity.

  • Higher wages improve worker efficiency
  • Reduces labor turnover
  • Can create unemployment

20. Coordination Failure

Coordination failures occur when economic agents cannot coordinate actions efficiently.

  • Low demand may persist
  • Firms hesitate to invest
  • Government intervention may help

21. Post Keynesian Economics

Post Keynesian economists expanded Keynesian ideas further.

  • Focus on uncertainty
  • Importance of financial markets
  • Demand drives long-run growth

22. Post Keynesian View on Money

Post Keynesians believe money is endogenous.

  • Banks create money through lending
  • Credit influences growth
  • Financial instability matters

23. Phillips Curve

The Phillips Curve shows relationship between inflation and unemployment.

  • Short-run trade-off exists
  • Lower unemployment may increase inflation
  • Important macroeconomic concept

24. Keynesian Phillips Curve

Keynesians believed stable trade-off exists between inflation and unemployment.

  • Higher inflation lowers unemployment
  • Government can manage demand
  • Policy can influence employment

25. Monetarist Phillips Curve

Milton Friedman argued the Phillips Curve trade-off exists only temporarily.

  • Long-run Phillips Curve is vertical
  • Natural rate of unemployment exists
  • Inflation expectations matter

26. New Classical Phillips Curve

New classical economists emphasized rational expectations.

  • People anticipate inflation
  • Expected inflation changes behavior
  • Policy becomes ineffective

27. New Keynesian Phillips Curve

New Keynesian Phillips Curve includes expectations and sticky prices.

  • Inflation depends on expectations
  • Price rigidity matters
  • Output gap affects inflation

28. Labor Demand

Labor demand refers to firmsâ€™ demand for workers.

  • Depends on productivity
  • Depends on wages
  • Derived from demand for goods

29. Labor Supply

Labor supply refers to willingness of workers to work.

  • Depends on wages
  • Higher wages may increase labor supply
  • Influenced by preferences and leisure

30. Labor Market Equilibrium

Equilibrium occurs where labor demand equals labor supply.

  • Determines wages
  • Determines employment level
  • Market forces influence equilibrium

31. Real Wage

Real wage measures purchasing power of wages.

  • Nominal wage adjusted for inflation
  • Important in labor analysis
  • Affects living standards

32. Natural Rate of Unemployment

The natural rate is unemployment existing even at equilibrium.

  • Includes frictional unemployment
  • Includes structural unemployment
  • Cannot be reduced permanently by policy

33. Adaptive Expectations

Adaptive expectations are formed based on past experiences.

  • People learn gradually
  • Past inflation influences future expectations
  • Used in monetarist models

34. Rational Expectations

Rational expectations use all available information.

  • People make informed forecasts
  • Economic agents are forward-looking
  • Used in new classical theory

35. Wage Rigidity

Wages may not adjust quickly in labor markets.

  • Contracts prevent quick changes
  • Labor unions influence wages
  • Can create unemployment

36. Price Rigidity

Prices may remain fixed even when demand changes.

  • Menu costs discourage price changes
  • Sticky prices affect output
  • Important in New Keynesian theory

37. Fiscal Policy

Fiscal policy uses government spending and taxation to stabilize economy.

  • Increases aggregate demand
  • Reduces unemployment
  • Influences output and growth

38. Monetary Policy

Monetary policy controls money supply and interest rates.

  • Managed by central bank
  • Controls inflation
  • Influences investment and output

39. Key Terms

  • Keynesian Economics: Theory emphasizing aggregate demand.
  • Monetarism: Theory emphasizing money supply.
  • Phillips Curve: Relationship between inflation and unemployment.
  • Rational Expectations: Expectations based on all available information.
  • Sticky Prices: Prices that adjust slowly.
  • Natural Rate of Unemployment: Equilibrium unemployment level.
