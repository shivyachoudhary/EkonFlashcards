Microeconomics – Theory

Intermediate Macroeconomics I

1. Introduction to Microeconomics I

Microeconomics studies behavior of individual consumers and firms in an economy.

  • Studies consumer behavior
  • Studies firm decisions
  • Analyzes allocation of resources

2. Consumer Theory

Consumer theory explains how consumers make choices to maximize satisfaction.

  • Consumers face limited income
  • Consumers choose among alternatives
  • Preferences influence decisions

3. Budget Constraint

Budget constraint shows combinations of goods a consumer can purchase with given income.

  • Income limits consumption
  • Higher income expands choices
  • Price changes affect budget line

4. Budget Line

The budget line represents all affordable combinations of two goods.

  • Slope depends on prices
  • Income shifts the line
  • Shows consumption possibilities

5. Changes in Budget Constraint

Budget constraints change due to income and price changes.

  • Income increase shifts line outward
  • Income decrease shifts line inward
  • Price changes rotate the line

6. Preferences

Preferences describe consumer tastes and choices among goods.

  • Consumers rank alternatives
  • Preferences guide decisions
  • Used in demand analysis

7. Assumptions of Preferences

Economists make assumptions about consumer preferences.

  • Completeness
  • Transitivity
  • Non-satiation

8. Indifference Curves

Indifference curves show combinations of goods giving equal satisfaction.

  • Downward sloping
  • Cannot intersect
  • Higher curves give higher utility

9. Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)

MRS measures willingness to substitute one good for another.

  • Represents trade-off between goods
  • Diminishes along indifference curve
  • Related to slope of curve

10. Utility

Utility measures satisfaction gained from consumption.

  • Consumers maximize utility
  • Utility depends on preferences
  • Important in consumer theory

11. Total Utility

Total utility is total satisfaction from consuming goods.

  • Increases with consumption
  • May rise at decreasing rate
  • Used in utility analysis

12. Marginal Utility

Marginal utility is additional satisfaction from consuming one more unit.

  • Usually decreases with consumption
  • Important in demand theory
  • Explains consumer choices

13. Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

Marginal utility decreases as consumption increases.

  • Additional units give less satisfaction
  • Explains downward demand curve
  • Important consumer principle

14. Consumer Choice

Consumers choose bundles maximizing utility within budget.

  • Utility maximization occurs at equilibrium
  • Budget limits choices
  • Preferences affect final choice

15. Consumer Equilibrium

Consumer equilibrium occurs where budget line touches highest indifference curve.

  • MRS equals price ratio
  • Maximum utility achieved
  • Optimal consumption bundle chosen

16. Revealed Preference Theory

Revealed preference theory studies choices based on actual consumer behavior.

  • Choices reveal preferences
  • Behavior indicates utility
  • Developed by Paul Samuelson

17. Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference

Consumers choose preferred bundles consistently.

  • Choices should not contradict
  • Consistency is important
  • Used in demand analysis

18. Slutsky Equation

The Slutsky equation separates price effect into substitution and income effects.

  • Explains consumer response to price changes
  • Substitution effect changes relative prices
  • Income effect changes purchasing power

19. Substitution Effect

Consumers substitute cheaper goods for expensive goods.

  • Occurs due to relative price change
  • Always negative for normal goods
  • Affects demand behavior

20. Income Effect

Income effect occurs because price changes affect real income.

  • Purchasing power changes
  • Demand may rise or fall
  • Depends on type of good

21. Normal Goods

Demand for normal goods rises when income increases.

  • Positive income effect
  • Common consumer goods
  • Consumption rises with income

22. Inferior Goods

Demand for inferior goods falls when income increases.

  • Negative income effect
  • Low-cost alternatives
  • Consumers switch to better goods

23. Giffen Goods

Giffen goods are special inferior goods where demand rises when price rises.

  • Strong negative income effect
  • Rare economic case
  • Violates normal demand law

24. Buying and Selling

Consumers may act as buyers or sellers depending on prices and endowments.

  • Price changes affect welfare
  • Consumers may gain or lose
  • Important in agricultural markets

25. Endowment

Endowment refers to resources or goods initially owned by consumers.

  • Determines budget possibilities
  • Affects market behavior
  • Important in exchange economy

26. Intertemporal Choice

Intertemporal choice studies decisions involving present and future consumption.

  • Consumers choose across time periods
  • Savings and borrowing matter
  • Interest rates affect decisions

27. Savings and Borrowing

Consumers save or borrow to smooth consumption over time.

  • Savings increase future consumption
  • Borrowing increases present consumption
  • Interest rates influence decisions

28. Present and Future Consumption

Consumers allocate income between current and future consumption.

  • Time preferences matter
  • Interest rates affect allocation
  • Important in investment decisions

29. Uncertainty

Consumers and firms make decisions under uncertain conditions.

  • Future outcomes may vary
  • Risk influences behavior
  • Probability matters in decisions

30. Risk and Probability

Risk involves uncertain outcomes with measurable probabilities.

  • Higher risk affects decisions
  • Probability measures likelihood
  • Important in insurance and finance

31. Expected Utility Theory

Expected utility theory explains choices under uncertainty.

  • Consumers maximize expected satisfaction
  • Probabilities influence choices
  • Used in decision theory

32. Risk Aversion

Risk-averse consumers prefer certainty over risky alternatives.

  • Avoid uncertain outcomes
  • Important in insurance markets
  • Common consumer behavior

33. Production Function

Production function shows relationship between inputs and output.

  • Labor and capital are important inputs
  • Technology affects production
  • Used in firm analysis

34. Short-Run Production Function

In short run, at least one factor remains fixed.

  • Fixed and variable inputs exist
  • Output changes with labor
  • Important in production analysis

35. Long-Run Production Function

In long run, all inputs are variable.

  • Firms adjust all factors
  • Scale becomes important
  • Production flexibility increases

36. Total Product

Total product is total output produced by inputs.

  • Output rises with more inputs
  • Used in production analysis
  • Related to productivity

37. Marginal Product

Marginal product is additional output from one extra unit of input.

  • Measures productivity of input
  • Usually rises then falls
  • Important in labor demand

38. Average Product

Average product measures output per unit of input.

  • Shows average productivity
  • Related to efficiency
  • Used in production analysis

39. Law of Diminishing Returns

Marginal product eventually decreases as more variable input is added.

  • Occurs in short run
  • Fixed factors limit production
  • Important production principle

40. Key Terms

  • Budget Constraint: Limit on consumer spending.
  • Utility: Satisfaction from consumption.
  • Indifference Curve: Equal satisfaction combinations.
  • MRS: Rate of substitution between goods.
  • Slutsky Equation: Separation of price effect.
  • Intertemporal Choice: Consumption across time.
  • Expected Utility: Utility under uncertainty.
  • Production Function: Relation between inputs and output.

41. Summary

Microeconomics I studies consumer behavior, uncertainty, intertemporal choice, and production decisions.

  • Consumers maximize utility within budget constraints.
  • Preferences and indifference curves explain choices.
  • Slutsky equation separates substitution and income effects.
  • Intertemporal choice studies consumption across time.
  • Expected utility explains decisions under uncertainty.
  • Production functions explain relationship between inputs and output.
  • Law of diminishing returns affects production efficiency.
