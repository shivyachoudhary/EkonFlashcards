Intermediate Macroeconomics I & II

1. Introduction to Macroeconomics

Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole. It focuses on national income, inflation, unemployment, growth, and overall economic performance.

  • Studies aggregate economic behavior
  • Analyzes economy-wide problems
  • Focuses on growth and stability

2. Scope of Macroeconomics

Macroeconomics covers major economic aggregates and policies.

  • National income
  • Inflation
  • Unemployment
  • Money and banking
  • Economic growth

3. National Income

National income measures the total value of goods and services produced in an economy during a specific period.

  • Measures economic performance
  • Helps in policy formulation
  • Indicates growth level

4. Concepts of National Income

Different concepts are used to measure national income.

  • GDP
  • GNP
  • NNP
  • Personal income
  • Disposable income

5. Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

GDP is the total value of goods and services produced within a country.

  • Measures domestic production
  • Important growth indicator
  • Widely used in economics

6. Gross National Product (GNP)

GNP includes income earned by citizens of a country.

  • Includes overseas earnings
  • Measures national production
  • Includes income from abroad

7. Net National Product (NNP)

NNP is obtained after deducting depreciation from GNP.

  • Measures net production
  • Accounts for wear and tear
  • Important for economic analysis

8. Personal Income

Personal income refers to income received by individuals and households.

  • Includes wages and salaries
  • Includes transfer payments
  • Used for consumption and savings

9. Disposable Income

Disposable income is income available after paying taxes.

  • Used for consumption
  • Used for savings
  • Important in demand analysis

10. Methods of Measuring National Income

National income can be measured using different methods.

  • Production method
  • Income method
  • Expenditure method

11. Circular Flow of Income

The circular flow shows movement of income and expenditure between sectors of the economy.

  • Households provide factors of production
  • Firms produce goods and services
  • Income and expenditure circulate continuously

12. Two-Sector Circular Flow Model

The two-sector model includes households and firms.

  • Households supply labor
  • Firms pay wages and salaries
  • Goods and services flow to households

13. Three-Sector Circular Flow Model

The three-sector model includes government along with households and firms.

  • Government collects taxes
  • Government spends on public services
  • Public expenditure affects income flow

14. Four-Sector Circular Flow Model

The four-sector model includes the foreign sector.

  • Exports bring income
  • Imports create outflow
  • International trade affects the economy

15. Leakages and Injections

Leakages reduce income flow while injections increase it.

  • Leakages include savings and taxes
  • Injections include investment and government spending
  • Balance maintains equilibrium

16. Money

Money is anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange.

  • Facilitates transactions
  • Removes difficulties of barter
  • Supports economic activity

17. Functions of Money

Money performs several important functions in an economy.

  • Medium of exchange
  • Store of value
  • Unit of account
  • Standard of deferred payments

18. Supply of Money

Money supply refers to the total money available in an economy.

  • Controlled by central bank
  • Affects inflation and interest rates
  • Influences economic growth

19. Demand for Money

Demand for money means the desire to hold money balances.

  • Transaction motive
  • Precautionary motive
  • Speculative motive

20. Inflation

Inflation means a continuous rise in the general price level.

  • Reduces purchasing power
  • Affects savings and investment
  • Measured using price indices

21. Unemployment

Unemployment occurs when people willing to work cannot find jobs.

  • Frictional unemployment
  • Structural unemployment
  • Cyclical unemployment

22. Keynesian Economics

Keynesian economics was developed by John Maynard Keynes. It emphasizes the role of aggregate demand in determining output and employment.

  • Government intervention is important
  • Demand determines economic activity
  • Useful during recession and unemployment

23. Keynesian Model

The Keynesian model explains how aggregate demand affects national income and employment.

  • Higher demand increases output
  • Low demand causes unemployment
  • Government spending can increase demand

24. Aggregate Demand in Keynesian Theory

Aggregate demand plays a central role in Keynesian economics.

  • Consumption affects demand
  • Investment affects production
  • Government expenditure increases demand
  • Demand influences employment levels

25. Consumption Function

The consumption function shows the relationship between income and consumption.

  • Consumption increases with income
  • People spend part of additional income
  • Savings also increase with income

26. Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)

MPC measures change in consumption due to change in income.

  • Value lies between 0 and 1
  • Higher MPC increases spending
  • Important in multiplier analysis

27. Multiplier Effect

The multiplier effect explains how an increase in investment or spending increases national income by a larger amount.

  • Initial spending creates additional income
  • Higher MPC creates a larger multiplier
  • Important in fiscal policy

28. Keynesian Unemployment

Keynes believed unemployment occurs because of insufficient aggregate demand.

  • Low demand reduces production
  • Firms reduce employment
  • Government spending can reduce unemployment

29. Importance of Keynesian Economics

Keynesian economics helped governments manage economic instability.

  • Useful during recessions
  • Supports active government policy
  • Influenced modern macroeconomics

30. IS Curve

The IS curve represents equilibrium in the goods market.

  • IS means Investment equals Savings
  • Shows relationship between income and interest rate
  • Downward sloping curve

31. LM Curve

The LM curve represents equilibrium in the money market.

  • LM means Liquidity equals Money supply
  • Shows money market equilibrium
  • Upward sloping curve

32. IS-LM Model

The IS-LM model explains simultaneous equilibrium in goods and money markets.

  • Combines IS and LM curves
  • Determines equilibrium income
  • Determines equilibrium interest rate

33. Aggregate Demand (AD)

Aggregate demand represents total demand for goods and services in an economy.

  • Includes consumption
  • Includes investment
  • Includes government expenditure
  • Includes net exports

34. Aggregate Supply (AS)

Aggregate supply represents total output produced in an economy.

  • Depends on production capacity
  • Affected by input costs
  • Influences price level

35. AD-AS Model

The AD-AS model explains output and price level determination.

  • AD curve slopes downward
  • AS curve slopes upward
  • Equilibrium determines output and prices

36. Equilibrium in AD-AS Model

Equilibrium occurs where aggregate demand equals aggregate supply.

  • Determines national output
  • Determines price level
  • Changes shift equilibrium point

37. Fiscal Policy

Fiscal policy refers to government expenditure and taxation policies.

  • Used to control the economy
  • Affects aggregate demand
  • Helps reduce unemployment

38. Monetary Policy

Monetary policy refers to control of money supply and interest rates.

  • Managed by central bank
  • Controls inflation
  • Influences investment and growth

39. Key Terms

  • GDP: Total value of goods and services produced domestically.
  • GNP: GDP plus income from abroad.
  • NNP: GNP minus depreciation.
  • Money Supply: Total money available in the economy.
  • IS Curve: Goods market equilibrium curve.
  • LM Curve: Money market equilibrium curve.
  • Aggregate Demand: Total demand in the economy.
  • Aggregate Supply: Total production in the economy.
  • MPC: Change in consumption due to change in income.
  • Multiplier: Process by which spending increases income by a larger amount.

40. Summary

Macroeconomics studies economy-wide issues such as national income, circular flow, money, inflation, unemployment, and growth.

  • National income measures economic performance.
  • Circular flow explains income and expenditure movement.
  • Money supports exchange, saving, and payment.
  • Keynesian economics emphasizes aggregate demand.
  • The Keynesian model explains income and employment determination.
  • IS-LM model explains goods and money market equilibrium.
  • AD-AS model explains output and price determination.
  • Fiscal and monetary policies help stabilize the economy.
