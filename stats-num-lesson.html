Mastering numericals

1. Mean

The mean represents the average value of a dataset.

  • Formula: Mean = Sum of observations ÷ Number of observations
  • Example: Mean of 10, 20, 30, 40 = 25
  • Used in economics, finance, and business analysis

2. Median

The median is the middle value of ordered data.

  • Arrange data in ascending order
  • Select middle observation
  • Example: Median of 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 = 15

3. Mode

The mode is the most frequently occurring value.

  • Useful for categorical data
  • Can have more than one mode
  • Example: Mode of 2, 3, 3, 4, 5 = 3

4. Range

Range measures the spread of data.

  • Formula: Range = Maximum − Minimum
  • Example: Range of 10, 20, 30 = 20
  • Simple measure of dispersion

5. Variance

Variance measures average squared deviation from the mean.

  • Higher variance means greater spread
  • Used in risk and uncertainty analysis
  • Important in probability and statistics

6. Standard Deviation

Standard deviation is the square root of variance.

  • Measures variability around mean
  • Low SD means clustered data
  • High SD means dispersed data

7. Probability

Probability measures likelihood of an event occurring.

  • Values lie between 0 and 1
  • 0 means impossible event
  • 1 means certain event

8. Coin Toss Probability

A coin has two equally likely outcomes.

  • Heads probability = 1/2
  • Tails probability = 1/2
  • Total probability = 1

9. Dice Probability

A fair die has six outcomes.

  • Probability of any one number = 1/6
  • Probability of even number = 3/6
  • Probability of odd number = 3/6

10. Conditional Probability

Conditional probability measures probability given another event has occurred.

  • Formula: P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B)
  • Used in forecasting and machine learning
  • Important in economic decision making

11. Bayes’ Theorem

Bayes’ theorem updates probability using new information.

  • Uses prior and posterior probability
  • Applied in AI and economics
  • Important in predictive analytics

12. Random Variables

Random variables depend on outcomes of random experiments.

  • Represented by X
  • Can be discrete or continuous
  • Used in probability models

13. Discrete Random Variables

Discrete variables take countable values.

  • Examples: number of students, number of cars
  • Used with PMF
  • Represented using bar graphs

14. Continuous Random Variables

Continuous variables take values in intervals.

  • Examples: income, height, weight
  • Used with PDF
  • Represented using smooth curves

15. PMF

Probability Mass Function is used for discrete variables.

  • Total probabilities sum to 1
  • Represented using bar charts
  • Shows exact probabilities

16. PDF

Probability Density Function is used for continuous variables.

  • Represented using smooth curves
  • Area under curve gives probability
  • Used in continuous distributions

17. CDF

Cumulative Distribution Function gives cumulative probability.

  • Formula: F(x) = P(X ≤ x)
  • Always increasing
  • Values range from 0 to 1

18. Bernoulli Distribution

Bernoulli distribution has only two outcomes.

  • Success or failure
  • Buy or not buy
  • Pass or fail

19. Binomial Distribution

Measures number of successes in repeated trials.

  • Fixed number of trials
  • Constant probability
  • Used in surveys and experiments

20. Geometric Distribution

Measures number of trials until first success.

  • Repeated independent trials
  • Constant success probability
  • Used in waiting-time analysis

21. Poisson Distribution

Models number of events occurring in fixed intervals.

  • Customer arrivals
  • Phone calls per hour
  • Traffic flow analysis

22. Normal Distribution

Normal distribution is symmetric and bell-shaped.

  • Mean equals median
  • Most observations lie near mean
  • Used widely in economics and finance

23. Exponential Distribution

Models waiting time between events.

  • Used in queue analysis
  • Customer arrival analysis
  • Reliability studies

24. Sampling

Sampling means selecting subset from population.

  • Reduces cost and time
  • Makes large studies manageable
  • Used in surveys and research

25. Random Sampling

Every individual has equal chance of selection.

  • Reduces bias
  • Simple method
  • Common in surveys

26. Stratified Sampling

Population is divided into groups before sampling.

  • Improves representation
  • Useful for heterogeneous populations
  • Used in social science research

27. Systematic Sampling

Every kth element is selected from a list.

  • Simple and fast
  • Useful in large datasets
  • Common in surveys

28. Histogram

Histogram shows frequency distribution of grouped data.

  • Shows shape of distribution
  • Used for continuous variables
  • Helps identify skewness

29. Scatter Plot

Scatter plot shows relationship between two variables.

  • Positive correlation
  • Negative correlation
  • No correlation

30. Box Plot

Box plots summarize distribution of data.

  • Shows quartiles and median
  • Detects outliers
  • Useful for comparison

31. Correlation

Correlation measures relationship between variables.

  • Positive relationship
  • Negative relationship
  • No relationship

32. Key Terms

  • Mean: Average value of data.
  • Median: Middle value of ordered data.
  • Mode: Most frequent value.
  • Variance: Average squared deviation from mean.
  • Standard Deviation: Square root of variance.
  • Probability: Likelihood of an event.
  • PMF: Probability Mass Function.
  • PDF: Probability Density Function.
  • CDF: Cumulative Distribution Function.

33. Summary

Numerical statistics focuses on calculations, probability, distributions, and data interpretation.

  • Mean, median, and mode measure central tendency.
  • Variance and standard deviation measure spread.
  • Probability measures uncertainty.
  • PMF, PDF, and CDF describe probability behavior.
  • Distributions help model real-world events.
  • Sampling helps analyze populations efficiently.
  • Graphs help visualize data and relationships.
