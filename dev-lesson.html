Economic History of India

Important concepts

1. Introduction to Development Economics

Development economics is a branch of economics that studies how countries improve economic conditions, living standards, education, healthcare, employment, and overall human welfare. It focuses mainly on developing and underdeveloped countries.

Economic development is broader than economic growth. Economic growth mainly refers to an increase in national income or GDP, while economic development includes improvements in health, education, equality, infrastructure, and quality of life.

  • Focuses on developing countries
  • Includes economic and social progress
  • Broader than economic growth

2. Economic Growth vs Economic Development

Economic growth refers to increase in national income while development includes improvement in quality of life.

  • Growth is quantitative concept.
  • Development is qualitative and quantitative concept.
  • Growth means increase in GDP or national income.
  • Development means improvement in living standards.
  • Growth mainly focuses on output whereas Development reduces poverty and inequality.
  • Development includes healthcare and education
  • Growth is a narrow concept whereas Development is a broader concept.

3. Characteristics of Developing Countries

Developing countries share common economic features.

  • Low per capita income
  • High population growth
  • Dependence on agriculture
  • High unemployment and underemployment
  • Low industrialization
  • Poverty and inequality
  • Low savings and investment
  • Poor infrastructure
  • Low literacy rate
  • Technological backwardness

4. Human Development Index (HDI)

HDI measures overall human development.

  • Published by UNDP
  • Includes education
  • Includes life expectancy
  • Includes standard of living

5. Poverty

Poverty means inability to satisfy basic human needs such as food, clothing, shelter, healthcare, and education.

  • Absolute poverty : Inability to meet minimum basic needs.
  • Relative poverty : Poverty measured relative to others in society.
  • Chronic poverty : Long-term poverty.
  • Situational poverty : Temporary poverty due to crises.

6. Causes of Poverty

Several economic and social factors create poverty.

  • Unemployment
  • Illiteracy
  • Rapid population growth
  • Low productivity
  • Unequal income distribution
  • Corruption

7. Poverty Trap

Poor people remain poor due to lack of resources and opportunities.

  • Low income
  • Low savings
  • Low investment
  • Low productivity

8. Unemployment

Unemployment occurs when people willing and able to work cannot find jobs.

  • Frictional unemployment : Temporary unemployment while changing jobs.
  • Structural unemployment : Occurs due to mismatch of skills.
  • Seasonal unemployment : Common in agriculture.
  • Disguised unemployment : Occurs during economic recessions.
  • Educated unemployment : More people employed than required.

9. Education and Development

Education improves productivity and economic growth.

  • Builds human capital
  • Reduces poverty
  • Creates employment opportunities
  • Promotes innovation

10. Human Capital

Human capital refers to the skills, knowledge, education, and health possessed by people.
Investment in education and health improves human capital.

  • Improves productivity
  • Increases economic efficiency
  • Encourages innovation

11. Sustainable Development

Development that satisfies present needs without harming future generations.

  • Environmental protection
  • Resource conservation
  • Long-term growth

12. Inclusive Growth

Economic growth that benefits all sections of society.

  • Reduces inequality
  • Creates employment
  • Improves living standards

13. Balanced Growth Theory

Given by Ragnar Nurkse: The theory states that simultaneous investment should occur in multiple sectors.

  • Investment in multiple sectors
  • Promotes industrialization
  • Expands markets

14. Unbalanced Growth Theory

Given by Albert Hirschman: The theory argues that development should begin in selected key sectors.

  • Development starts in selected sectors
  • Efficient use of resources
  • Strategic investment approach

15. Big Push Theory

Given by Paul Rosenstein-Rodan.

  • Large-scale investment is necessary
  • Breaks underdevelopment cycle
  • Supports industrialization

16. Critical Minimum Effort Theory

Given by Harvey Leibenstein.

  • Small efforts are insufficient
  • Large development effort required
  • Helps break poverty traps

17. Lewis Dual Sector Model

Given by W. Arthur Lewis.

  • Labor shifts from agriculture to industry
  • Industrialization drives growth
  • Surplus labor exists in agriculture

18. Harrod-Domar Growth Model

Growth depends on savings and investment.

  • Focus on capital formation
  • Higher savings increase growth
  • Uses capital-output ratio

19. Solow Growth Model

Developed by Robert Solow.

  • Technology drives long-run growth
  • Includes labor and capital
  • Focuses on productivity

Developing countries depend on developed countries economically.

  • Economic exploitation

22. Modernization Theory

Development occurs through industrialization and modernization.

  • Adoption of technology
  • Institutional reforms
  • Industrial development

23. Kuznets Inverted U Hypothesis

Income inequality first rises and later falls during development.

  • Given by Simon Kuznets
  • Shape resembles inverted U
  • Explains inequality trends

26. Gender and Development

Women empowerment is important for development.

  • Equal wages
  • Education for women
  • Healthcare access
  • Political participation

27. Population and Development

Population affects economic development.

  • Large labor force
  • Pressure on resources
  • Can increase unemployment

28. Agriculture and Development

Agriculture supports employment and food security.

  • Irrigation
  • Mechanization
  • Improved productivity

29. Industrialization and Development

Industrialization promotes economic growth.

  • Higher productivity
  • Urbanization
  • Employment generation

30. Globalization and Development

Globalization integrates economies through trade and investment.

  • Technology transfer
  • Foreign investment
  • Increased trade

31. Microfinance

Provides small loans to poor households and businesses.

  • Supports entrepreneurship
  • Reduces poverty
  • Encourages self-employment

32. Role of Government in Development

Government supports economic development through policies and programs.

  • Healthcare programs
  • Infrastructure development
  • Education spending
  • Poverty reduction schemes

33. Environmental Issues in Development

Rapid development can create environmental problems.

  • Pollution
  • Deforestation
  • Climate change
  • Resource depletion

34. Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

Global goals introduced by the United Nations in 2015.

  • No poverty
  • Quality education
  • Gender equality
  • Climate action

35. Important Economists

Major contributors to development economics.

  • Adam Smith: Division of labor and economic growth.
  • Arthur Lewis: Dual sector model.
  • Amartya Sen: Capability approach and human development.
  • Simon Kuznets: Income inequality hypothesis.
  • Ragnar Nurkse: Balanced growth theory.

  • HDI: Measures development through education, health, and income.
  • Poverty Trap: A cycle where low income leads to low savings, low investment, and low productivity.
  • Human Capital: Skills, education, knowledge, and health of people.
  • Gini Coefficient: A measure of income inequality.
  • Sustainable Development: Development without harming future generations.
  • Inclusive Growth: Growth that benefits all sections of society.

37. Summary

Development economics studies how countries improve income, living standards, education, health, employment, and overall welfare. It is broader than economic growth because it focuses not only on GDP but also on human development and quality of life.

  • Development includes growth, equality, education, health, and employment.
  • Poverty and unemployment are major barriers to development.
  • Education and health improve human capital and productivity.
  • HDI, GDP, per capita income, and Gini coefficient are important measures of development.
  • Lewis model explains movement of labour from agriculture to industry.
  • Harrod-Domar model focuses on savings and investment.
  • Solow model highlights technology as a source of long-run growth.
  • Kuznets hypothesis explains the relationship between growth and inequality.
  • Amartya Senâ€™s capability approach focuses on freedom, opportunity, education, and health.
  • Sustainable and inclusive growth are important for fair and long-term development.
