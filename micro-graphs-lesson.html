Intermediate Microeconomics II

1. Introduction to Microeconomics II

Microeconomics II studies market structures, strategic behavior, welfare economics, and market failures.

  • Studies firm behavior
  • Analyzes market structures
  • Focuses on welfare and efficiency

2. Perfect Competition

Perfect competition is a market structure with many buyers and sellers selling identical products.

  • Large number of firms
  • Homogeneous products
  • Free entry and exit
  • Firms are price takers

3. Features of Perfect Competition

Perfect competition has specific assumptions about markets.

  • Perfect information
  • No barriers to entry
  • Uniform market price
  • No control over price by firms

4. Short-Run Equilibrium in Perfect Competition

Firms maximize profit where marginal cost equals marginal revenue.

  • Firms may earn supernormal profit
  • Firms may incur losses
  • Price determined by market demand and supply

5. Long-Run Equilibrium in Perfect Competition

In long run, firms earn only normal profits.

  • Free entry removes extra profits
  • Loss-making firms exit market
  • Market reaches equilibrium

6. Monopoly

Monopoly is a market structure with a single seller producing a unique product.

  • Single producer exists
  • No close substitutes
  • High barriers to entry
  • Firm has price-making power

7. Features of Monopoly

Monopolies possess significant market power.

  • Control over price
  • Restricted output
  • Limited competition
  • Higher profits possible

8. Monopoly Equilibrium

A monopolist maximizes profit where marginal revenue equals marginal cost.

  • Price exceeds marginal cost
  • Output lower than perfect competition
  • Can create deadweight loss

9. Price Discrimination

Price discrimination occurs when same product is sold at different prices to different consumers.

  • Requires market power
  • Requires market separation
  • Prevents resale between consumers

10. First-Degree Price Discrimination

Consumers are charged maximum willingness to pay.

  • Perfect price discrimination
  • Entire consumer surplus captured
  • Rare in practice

11. Second-Degree Price Discrimination

Prices vary according to quantity consumed.

  • Bulk discounts common
  • Different pricing blocks used
  • Consumers self-select plans

12. Third-Degree Price Discrimination

Different consumer groups are charged different prices.

  • Student discounts
  • Senior citizen discounts
  • Market segmentation important

13. Oligopoly

Oligopoly is a market structure dominated by a few large firms.

  • Firms are interdependent
  • Strategic behavior important
  • Barriers to entry exist

14. Features of Oligopoly

Oligopolistic firms consider reactions of competitors.

  • Few large firms dominate
  • Products may be homogeneous or differentiated
  • Price rigidity may exist

15. Cournot Model

Cournot model assumes firms compete by choosing output quantities.

  • Firms decide quantity simultaneously
  • Competitors’ output affects profits
  • Strategic interaction important

16. Cournot Equilibrium

Cournot equilibrium occurs when each firm chooses optimal output given rivals’ outputs.

  • No firm wants to change output
  • Mutual best responses achieved
  • Stable strategic equilibrium

17. Bertrand Model

Bertrand model assumes firms compete through prices.

  • Firms choose prices simultaneously
  • Lower price attracts consumers
  • Price competition may reduce profits

18. Bertrand Equilibrium

Bertrand equilibrium occurs when firms cannot gain by changing prices.

  • Prices may equal marginal cost
  • Strong competition exists
  • Profits may become very low

19. Stackelberg Model

Stackelberg model involves leader and follower firms.

  • Leader chooses output first
  • Follower reacts later
  • Strategic advantage to leader

20. Stackelberg Equilibrium

Stackelberg equilibrium occurs when leader and follower optimize decisions.

  • Leader anticipates follower reaction
  • Output differs from Cournot model
  • Leader earns higher profit

21. Cartels

Cartels are agreements among firms to reduce competition.

  • Firms cooperate on prices or output
  • Increase collective profits
  • Often illegal in many countries

22. Game Theory

Game theory studies strategic interactions among firms and individuals.

  • Strategies affect outcomes
  • Competitor behavior matters
  • Used in oligopoly analysis

23. Nash Equilibrium

Nash equilibrium occurs when no player benefits from changing strategy alone.

  • Stable strategic outcome
  • Used in game theory
  • Important in oligopoly models

24. General Equilibrium

General equilibrium studies simultaneous equilibrium in all markets.

  • Markets are interconnected
  • Prices coordinate economy
  • Resources allocated efficiently

25. Walrasian Equilibrium

Walrasian equilibrium occurs when all markets clear simultaneously.

  • Demand equals supply
  • Prices adjust automatically
  • Economy reaches balance

26. Welfare Economics

Welfare economics studies allocation of resources and social well-being.

  • Focuses on economic efficiency
  • Analyzes social welfare
  • Evaluates policy outcomes

27. Pareto Efficiency

Pareto efficiency occurs when no one can be made better off without making someone else worse off.

  • Efficient allocation achieved
  • No waste of resources
  • Important welfare criterion

28. Social Welfare

Social welfare refers to overall well-being of society.

  • Depends on resource allocation
  • Influenced by income distribution
  • Important in public policy

29. Market Failure

Market failure occurs when markets fail to allocate resources efficiently.

  • Externalities create inefficiency
  • Public goods may be underprovided
  • Government intervention may help

30. Externalities

Externalities occur when actions affect third parties.

  • May be positive or negative
  • Markets may ignore social costs
  • Can create inefficiency

31. Positive Externalities

Positive externalities create benefits for others.

  • Education improves society
  • Vaccination benefits community
  • Market may underproduce such goods

32. Negative Externalities

Negative externalities impose costs on others.

  • Pollution harms society
  • Traffic congestion affects others
  • Government regulation may be needed

33. Public Goods

Public goods are goods available to everyone without exclusion.

  • Non-rival in consumption
  • Non-excludable
  • Private markets may fail to provide them

34. Free Rider Problem

People may enjoy public goods without paying for them.

  • Creates underproduction problem
  • Common in public goods
  • Government provision often required

35. Private Goods

Private goods are rival and excludable.

  • Consumed individually
  • Market prices determine allocation
  • Examples include food and clothing

36. Mixed Goods

Some goods have characteristics of both public and private goods.

  • Education and healthcare examples
  • Government involvement common
  • Partial market provision possible

37. Government Intervention

Government may intervene to correct market failures.

  • Taxes and subsidies used
  • Regulations imposed
  • Public goods provided

38. Subsidies and Taxes

Governments use subsidies and taxes to influence behavior.

  • Subsidies encourage positive activities
  • Taxes reduce harmful activities
  • Used to correct externalities

39. Key Terms

  • Monopoly: Single seller market.
  • Oligopoly: Market dominated by few firms.
  • Price Discrimination: Selling same product at different prices.
  • Cournot Equilibrium: Quantity competition equilibrium.
  • Bertrand Equilibrium: Price competition equilibrium.
  • Stackelberg Model: Leader-follower competition model.
  • Pareto Efficiency: Efficient resource allocation.
  • Externality: Third-party effect of economic activity.
  • Public Goods: Non-rival and non-excludable goods.

40. Summary

Microeconomics II studies market structures, strategic competition, welfare economics, and market failures.

  • Perfect competition has many firms and free entry.
  • Monopoly gives market power to one seller.
  • Oligopoly involves strategic interaction among firms.
  • Cournot, Bertrand, and Stackelberg models explain oligopoly behavior.
  • General equilibrium studies interconnected markets.
  • Welfare economics evaluates efficiency and social welfare.
  • Externalities and public goods create market failures.
  • Government intervention may improve efficiency.
