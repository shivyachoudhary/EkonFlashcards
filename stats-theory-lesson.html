Concept and Formulae

1. Introduction to Statistics

Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data.

  • Helps in decision making
  • Used in economics and business
  • Analyzes inflation, GDP, unemployment, and income

2. Types of Statistics

Statistics is divided into descriptive and inferential statistics.

  • Descriptive statistics summarizes data
  • Inferential statistics draws conclusions from samples
  • Used in forecasting and estimation

3. Data and Variables

Data is information collected for analysis.

  • Primary data is collected directly
  • Secondary data is collected from existing sources
  • Variables may be qualitative or quantitative

4. Qualitative and Quantitative Variables

Variables are characteristics that can take different values.

  • Qualitative variables represent categories
  • Quantitative variables represent numbers
  • Examples include income, gender, and education

5. Discrete and Continuous Variables

Quantitative variables are divided into discrete and continuous variables.

  • Discrete variables are countable
  • Continuous variables take values in intervals
  • Examples: number of students and income

6. Measures of Central Tendency

Central tendency describes the center of data.

  • Mean is the average value
  • Median is the middle value
  • Mode is the most frequent value

7. Mean

The mean is the average value of a dataset.

  • Add all observations
  • Divide by total number of observations
  • Widely used in economics and business

8. Median

The median is the middle value of ordered data.

  • Useful when data contains outliers
  • Represents central location
  • Often used in income studies

9. Mode

The mode is the most frequently occurring value.

  • Useful for categorical data
  • Can have more than one mode
  • Shows most common observation

10. Measures of Dispersion

Dispersion shows how spread out the data is.

  • Range
  • Variance
  • Standard deviation

11. Range

Range measures the difference between maximum and minimum values.

  • Range = Maximum âˆ’ Minimum
  • Simple measure of spread
  • Easy to calculate

12. Variance

Variance measures average squared deviation from the mean.

  • Measures variability
  • Higher variance means greater spread
  • Important in risk analysis

13. Standard Deviation

Standard deviation is the square root of variance.

  • Measures spread around mean
  • Low value means clustered data
  • High value means dispersed data

14. Probability

Probability measures the likelihood of an event occurring.

  • Values lie between 0 and 1
  • 0 means impossible event
  • 1 means certain event

15. Conditional Probability

Conditional probability measures probability given another event has occurred.

  • Used in forecasting
  • Important in economics and AI
  • Applied in decision making

16. Bayesâ€™ Theorem

Bayesâ€™ theorem updates probability using new information.

  • Uses prior and posterior probability
  • Applied in machine learning
  • Useful in economic forecasting

17. Random Variables

A random variable takes values depending on random experiments.

  • Represented by X
  • Can be discrete or continuous
  • Used in probability models

18. Discrete Random Variables

Discrete random variables take countable values.

  • Examples include number of students
  • Used with PMF
  • Represented using bar charts

19. Continuous Random Variables

Continuous random variables take values within intervals.

  • Examples include income and height
  • Used with PDF
  • Represented using smooth curves

20. PMF, PDF, and CDF

Probability functions describe probability behavior.

  • PMF is used for discrete variables
  • PDF is used for continuous variables
  • CDF gives cumulative probability

21. Bernoulli Distribution

Bernoulli distribution has two outcomes: success or failure.

  • Buy or not buy
  • Pass or fail
  • Simple probability model

22. Binomial Distribution

Binomial distribution measures successes in repeated trials.

  • Fixed number of trials
  • Constant probability
  • Used in surveys and experiments

23. Hypergeometric Distribution

Used when sampling is done without replacement.

  • Probability changes after selection
  • Used in quality control
  • Finite population model

24. Geometric Distribution

Measures trials until first success.

  • Repeated independent trials
  • Useful in waiting problems
  • Constant success probability

25. Negative Binomial Distribution

Measures trials required for multiple successes.

  • Extension of geometric distribution
  • Used in reliability studies
  • Common in analytics

26. Poisson Distribution

Models number of events in fixed intervals.

  • Phone calls per hour
  • Customer arrivals
  • Traffic analysis

27. Uniform Distribution

All values in an interval have equal probability.

  • Equal likelihood
  • Simple continuous distribution
  • Used in simulations

28. Normal Distribution

Normal distribution is bell-shaped and symmetric.

  • Mean equals median
  • Many economic variables follow it
  • Examples include income and test scores

29. Exponential Distribution

Models waiting time between events.

  • Used in queue analysis
  • Customer arrival studies
  • Reliability analysis

30. Sampling

Sampling means selecting a subset from a population.

  • Reduces cost and time
  • Makes studies manageable
  • Helps estimate population characteristics

31. Random Sampling

Every individual has equal chance of selection.

  • Reduces bias
  • Simple sampling technique
  • Used in surveys

32. Stratified Sampling

Population is divided into groups before sampling.

  • Improves representation
  • Useful for heterogeneous populations
  • Common in research studies

33. Systematic Sampling

Every kth element is selected from a list.

  • Simple to apply
  • Useful in large datasets
  • Widely used in surveys

34. Histogram

A histogram shows frequency distribution of data.

  • Represents grouped data
  • Useful for continuous variables
  • Shows distribution shape

35. Scatter Plot

Scatter plots show relationships between two variables.

  • Positive correlation
  • Negative correlation
  • No correlation

36. Box Plot

Box plots summarize data distribution.

  • Shows median and quartiles
  • Detects outliers
  • Useful for comparing distributions

37. Correlation

Correlation measures relationship between variables.

  • Positive correlation
  • Negative correlation
  • No correlation

38. Applications of Statistics in Economics

Statistics is widely used in economics.

  • GDP analysis
  • Inflation measurement
  • Unemployment studies
  • Economic forecasting

39. Key Terms

  • Statistics: Science of collecting and analyzing data.
  • Mean: Average value of data.
  • Median: Middle value of ordered data.
  • Mode: Most frequent value.
  • Variance: Average squared deviation from mean.
  • Standard Deviation: Square root of variance.
  • Probability: Likelihood of an event.
  • PMF: Probability mass function for discrete variables.
  • PDF: Probability density function for continuous variables.
  • CDF: Cumulative distribution function.

40. Summary

Statistics helps collect, organize, analyze, and interpret data for decision making. It includes descriptive and inferential statistics, probability, distributions, random variables, and sampling methods.

  • Mean, median, and mode describe central tendency.
  • Variance and standard deviation measure spread.
  • Probability measures uncertainty.
  • Random variables may be discrete or continuous.
  • PMF, PDF, and CDF describe probability behavior.
  • Probability distributions model real-world events.
  • Sampling helps study large populations efficiently.
  • Graphs help visualize relationships and distributions.
